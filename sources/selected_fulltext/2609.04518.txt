harness. 1 Introduction Coding agents act through production harnesses that choose prompts, tools, observations,
context-management rules, retries, and control flow ( Yang et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2025a ; Gauthier, 2024 ; Qwen Team, 2025a ) . Recent systems train policies through these
harnesses, sometimes using several harnesses or harness configurations for one policy ( Xu and others, 2026 ; Yu and others, 2026 ; Kimi Team, 2026 ; Song et al., 2026 ) . The phrase multi-harness RL does not specify how those harnesses interact during credit assignment. Exposure does not determine the grouping rule. When several harnesses attempt the same task,
their outcomes may be compared directly or normalized separately. HarnessX groups same-task
traces across successive harness and model versions, so the traces compete inside one GRPO group ( Chen and others, 2026 ) . ClawGym II treats each task–harness pair as the optimization unit instead:
the harnesses share a policy and minibatch, but reward normalization remains separate ( Song et al., 2026 ) . Because these systems also differ in exposure, rollout generation,
training regime, and evaluation, their end-to-end results do not isolate the grouping boundary. We isolate that boundary in repository-level coding (Figure 1 ). Four production
harnesses collect a frozen trajectory corpus on a shared SWE-Gym pool ( Pan et al., 2025 ) . From the same Qwen3-8B ( Qwen Team, 2025b ) supervised warm start, the two primary RL arms replay identical records,
tokens, rewards, loss masks, and update budget. Within forms GRPO ( Shao et al., 2024 ) groups inside each task–harness pair. Cross ( PlainCross in the released artifacts) pools all harnesses for the task. We evaluate
every resulting checkpoint with one sealed per-instance oracle on the four source harnesses and
on a minimal harness absent from training. The comparison holds experience diversity fixed and
changes only cross-harness credit assignment. Figure 1: Controlled multi-harness training and harness-swap evaluation. Four production
harnesses collect trajectories and rewards from the same SWE-Gym task pool; the frozen manifest
holds records, tokens, and rewards fixed across the primary arms. Cross pools same-task traces
across harnesses when constructing GRPO groups, whereas Within assigns credit separately within
each task–harness pair. Checkpoints are graded by the same sealed oracle on the source
harnesses and an unseen minimal harness. Source-profile changes combine portable improvement
with configuration adaptation; held-out changes isolate the component that survives the
harness swap. The evaluation harness, not the training recipe, is the largest effect in the study. Letting
harnesses compete for credit changes which trajectories carry gradient and leaves everything
downstream of that in place: the held-out score, and the policy’s action distribution inside
each harness. Seeing several harnesses and comparing them for credit are separate
interventions , and the second does not by itself make the learned policy harness-independent. Contributions. • A controlled recipe-by-harness matrix under one sealed oracle. The evaluation harness
moves solve rate by a factor of 4.3 where the training recipe moves it by
1.16, and harness strength differs between model families. • A same-data, same-budget experiment that isolates the GRPO grouping boundary across four
production coding harnesses and scores every checkpoint under a harness held out of training.
Cross and Within are indistinguishable there, with or without re-collecting the data on-policy,
and both place their largest gains on the same source harness. • A direct measurement of the harness identity each credit rule leaves in the advantage,

what that identity changes downstream: the same held-out score and the same action distribution
inside each harness either way. 2 Related work Harnesses as an evaluation variable. Harness-Bench, standardized leaderboards, and configuration-level audits show that agent scores,
costs, and failures depend on model–harness pairing ( Yao et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ; Kapoor et al., 2025 ; Kapoor et al., 2024 ) . The same
concern motivates agent suites that fix the interface as part of the benchmark ( Liu et al., 2024 ; Mialon et al., 2023 ; Zhou et al., 2024a ; Yao et al., 2025 ) and audits of how much
of a reported score is a property of the benchmark rather than the model ( Liang et al., 2023 ; Dehghani et al., 2021 ; Singh et al., 2025 ; Biderman et al., 2024 ) . We measure that heterogeneity on our own checkpoints (Section 4 ) and use it to pose a training question. Harness-native reinforcement learning. Polar, Tmax, OpenForgeRL, and related systems make policy optimization through production
harnesses practical and report substantial native-harness gains ( Xu and others, 2026 ; Ivison et al., 2026 ; Yu and others, 2026 ) . For repository-level coding specifically, SWE-RL and
SWE-Gym establish the outcome-reward recipe we replay ( Wei et al., 2025 ; Pan et al., 2025 ) , and
parallel lines train agents through device, web and search interfaces ( Bai et al., 2024 ; Qi et al., 2025 ; Jin et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2025b ) . They study infrastructure,
scaling, and native-harness performance. Our question is how outcomes from several native
harnesses should interact in the relative-credit estimator. Multi-harness training and transfer. Kimi K3, OpenForgeRL, and other frontier recipes vary heterogeneous harness exposure as part of a
larger post-training stack ( Kimi Team, 2026 ; Yu and others, 2026 ; Huang and others, 2026 ; NVIDIA, 2026 ) .
HarnessX pools same-task traces across evolving harness versions ( Chen and others, 2026 ) , ClawGym
II normalizes within each task–harness pair before mixed gradient updates ( Song et al., 2026 ) , and harness design has begun to be studied as a post-training variable in
its own right ( Kim et al., 2026 ; Foundation Model Team, Li Auto Inc., 2026 ) . That disagreement motivates a comparison
in which the trajectory manifest and update budget stay fixed and only the grouping boundary
moves, with source-configuration effects separated from held-out portability. Where credit is assigned. Most work on credit assignment in large language model (LLM) RL refines the resolution of the
signal: per-step value
estimates ( Kazemnejad et al., 2024 ) , hierarchical turn-level objectives ( Zhou et al., 2024b ; Feng et al., 2025 ) , hindsight reassignment over long horizons ( Tan and others, 2026 ) , or process supervision that scores intermediate steps ( Uesato et al., 2022 ; Lightman et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2024 ) . A second line asks
what the group-relative baseline itself normalizes away, and corrects its length and difficulty
bias ( Liu et al., 2025b ; Yu et al., 2025 ; Ahmadian et al., 2024 ) . Both take the set of trajectories that
compete for credit as given. We vary that set instead: the resolution of the reward is fixed at
one binary outcome per episode, and only the boundary of the comparison group moves. What RL adds over the starting policy. Whether outcome-reward RL creates capability or reweights what the base model already does is
contested ( Yue et al., 2025 ; Chu et al., 2025 ; Zeng et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2025a ; Guo et al., 2025 ) , and the
answer depends on how capability is measured. Spurious or misspecified rewards can move a score
without moving the underlying skill ( Shao et al., 2025 ; Skalse et al., 2022 ) , and RLHF narrows
diversity while raising the headline number ( Kirk et al., 2024 ; Singhal et al., 2023 ) . Our
setting adds a second axis to that question. Even granting that a gain is real on the harness that
produced the data, it may be a property of the model–harness pair rather than of the model. Evaluating under an unseen harness separates the two, and that measurement is what the rest of

solve rate into the advantage. Neither change reaches the held-out score or the action
distribution inside a harness, and for both rules the largest positive point estimate sits on
the harness family that produced the data. The pattern, whichever grouping boundary is used, is configuration adaptation without portable capability : gains of the kind a deployment on
the source configurations can use, and which only a harness swap distinguishes from
internalization. Scope of the result. The design asks one question: with exposure held fixed, what does comparing rewards across
harnesses add? Systems that report multi-harness gains also use larger task pools ( Yang et al., 2025 ) , train longer ( Liu et al., 2025a ) , combine supervision stages ( Lambert et al., 2024 ) , or co-evolve the harness ( Ivison et al., 2026 ; Yu and others, 2026 ; Kimi Team, 2026 ; Chen and others, 2026 ; Song et al., 2026 ) . Each is a separate
intervention from the one isolated here. Evaluation recommendation. A multi-harness RL report should state which harnesses generated experience, whether advantage
normalization crosses harness boundaries, the source-harness deltas, and the deltas under at
least one unseen harness. The first two specify the algorithm; the latter two separate source
adaptation from portable capability. 8 Conclusion The harness a coding agent runs through moves its solve rate by a factor of
4.3; the recipe that trained it moves it by 1.16. Multi-harness
exposure and cross-harness credit assignment are distinct interventions. Holding the first fixed
while varying the second alters the learning signal, and the harness identity it carries, but
not the held-out score, even when half the training data is re-collected on-policy. The
policy’s action distribution is set by the harness it runs in, not by the credit rule. The gains
that replaying multi-harness experience does produce sit on the training harnesses, not on the
held-out one, and comparing harnesses for credit does not change that.
Multi-harness RL studies should report the grouping boundary and
test portability under an unseen interface. 9 Limitations Every estimate other than the Within and Cross held-out levels rests on one checkpoint, and
the source harnesses were evaluated at two attempts per task. Absolute solve rates are low
( 1 1 – 10 % 10\% ) and the endpoint is binary. Training covers one model family ( Qwen Team, 2025b ; Hui et al., 2024 ) , one budget, four source harnesses, one round of

checkpoint per model, under context budgets that differ between the two families, and a

it. Multilingual, test-generation and contamination-hardened variants of the benchmark ( Zan et al., 2025 ; Mündler et al., 2024 ; Aleithan et al., 2024 ) , and the

paired per-task difference is bootstrapped by resampling tasks (10,000 draws, fixed seeds),
and two-sided 95% percentile intervals are reported [ Efron and Tibshirani, 1994 ] . Resampling the
task rather than the episode is the cluster-robust choice for repeated measurements on the same
task [ Cameron and Miller, 2015 , Agarwal et al., 2021 ] . Multiplicity. The six held-out contrasts were ordered and frozen before the
checkpoints existed, and the seen contrast carries a Bonferroni-corrected 99 % 99\% interval over
the five recipe comparisons (Table 3 ) [ Holm, 1979 ] . Completeness gate. A
cell enters analysis only at ≥ 95 % {\geq}95\% graded coverage. The analysis aborts (exit nonzero)
rather than degrades below the gate. The source matrix attempted 24,000 episodes at
99.1% verdict coverage, and the held-out rows reach 98.0 98.0 – 99.7 % 99.7\% at k = 4 k{=}4 and 98.6 98.6 – 99.3 % 99.3\% at k = 8 k{=}8 (Tables 1 and 2 ). Run variance. Identical reruns flip ∼ 6 % {\sim}6\% of per-task outcomes (continuous-batching nondeterminism, with
episode seeds marking provenance rather than determinism), motivating k = 4 k{=}4 on the weak-ReAct probe and
task-clustered paired inference throughout. A flat “ ± 6 ​ pp {\pm}6\,\mathrm{pp} ” discount would be wrong in
both directions: the aggregate impact of rerun noise shrinks as ∼ 0.06 / n {\sim}\sqrt{0.06/n} with
benchmark size, so what the flip rate rules out is specifically single-run, small- n n comparisons, not any difference under six points. Benchmark-level variance of this kind is well
documented [ Bouthillier et al., 2021 , Madaan et al., 2024 , Miller, 2024 ] , as is the
subset-selection effect that follows from it [ Dehghani et al., 2021 ] . Every reported number therefore uses the frozen 500 500 -task roster rather than any subset of
it. Seed variation. Re-training Within and Cross under two further seeds moved each
arm’s held-out level by 0.42 to 0.45 pp \,\mathrm{pp} (Table 2 );
the pooled three-seed contrast is in Table 3 . Detection floors. MDE 80 figures recovered from the interval widths under a normal
